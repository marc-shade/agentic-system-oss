# Explain-As: Creative Constraint Forcing Function

**Usage**: `/explain-as [domain] [topic]`

**Examples**:
- `/explain-as kitchen "how React hooks work"`
- `/explain-as sports "distributed systems consistency"`
- `/explain-as ocean "memory management in Rust"`
- `/explain-as construction "neural network architectures"`

---

## Your Task

Explain **{{arg2}}** using ONLY **{{arg1}}** analogies and vocabulary.

## Rules (Absolute)

1. **Every concept** must map to the {{arg1}} domain
2. **No abstract technical terms** - only {{arg1}} vocabulary allowed
3. **The constraint is absolute** - find creative mappings, no exceptions
4. **If stuck, get weirder** - the forced constraint reveals unexpected insights
5. **Make it concrete** - use specific {{arg1}} objects, actions, processes

## Why This Works

Arbitrary constraints force your brain to find novel connections. The limitation becomes a feature, not a bug. You'll discover explanations that wouldn't emerge from standard technical language.

## Example Quality

Bad: "React hooks are like kitchen tools that... but technically they're closures that capture state..."
Good: "React hooks are like prep stations in a professional kitchen. useState is your mise en place - ingredients prepped and ready. useEffect is the cleanup crew that runs after service. Custom hooks are your signature recipes that combine techniques..."

The constraint is the ENTIRE value proposition. Embrace it fully.

---

**Psychological Trick #5**: Fake constraints force creative thinking by limiting your available vocabulary/concepts, which forces novel pattern matching and analogical reasoning.
